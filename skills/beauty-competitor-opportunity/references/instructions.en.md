# Competitor and Opportunity Analysis: Professional Instructions

## Objective

Use verifiable competitor facts, user-problem signals, and entry barriers to support one defined product or packaging decision. Research is not a source dump, and an unobserved competitor action is not automatically an attractive opportunity.

## Define the research basis

State the target market, category or format, user or occasion, price and channel basis, research cut-off date, and decision to support. If no concrete decision question exists, propose a research scope before collecting broadly.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. Official product pages, instructions, regulatory databases, and formal brand disclosures for identity, specifications, and public claims.
2. Retail pages with identifiable channel and date for in-market variants and price snapshots; separate promotion from regular price.
3. Industry research with a stated method for market structure or audience signals; check publication date and sample.
4. Reviews and social content for problem language and occasions; record sample, channel, and bias and do not use one item as proof of an opportunity.

## Normalize comparisons

- Confirm that products belong to the same market, comparable format, and current sellable version.
- For price, record currency, tax basis, capacity, date, channel, and promotion state. Show raw values and formula for per-volume comparison.
- Keep public claims, ingredient or technology facts, sensory experience, packaging structure, capacity, and refill system in separate fields.
- Distinguish not observed, explicitly absent, and not verifiable.

## Opportunity decision

A recommended opportunity requires evidence of a repeated or material user problem, inadequate competitor resolution, a solution path that fits the target brand or capability, and a testable commercial or technical barrier. Record support and counter-evidence for each.

When evidence is insufficient, output research leads, missing data, and recommended interviews or tests—not a firm entry conclusion.

## Completion check

- Every major conclusion has a locatable source; inferences are labelled.
- Variants, products, and promotional prices are not mixed.
- One extreme review is not amplified into a user truth.
- Recommendations state the opportunity, barrier, counter-evidence, and validation step.
