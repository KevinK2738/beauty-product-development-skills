# Beauty Product Development Brief: Professional Instructions

## Objective

Turn product intent into a definition that product, R&D, packaging, supply, and marketing can review together. The brief defines what should be developed, why, under which constraints, and how it will be validated. It does not prove that the targets have been achieved.

## Minimum inputs

Where possible, establish the target user and occasion, market and channel, product format, core experience or problem, size and price tier, target timing, and non-negotiable brand or commercial constraints. Missing fields may remain open, but state the affected decision and owner.

## Definition method

- Translate words such as premium, fresh, gentle, effective, and portable into observable experience, appearance, operation, or validation questions.
- Separate core value, supporting features, and dispensable features; not every request is a top priority.
- Describe formula direction as target properties, exclusions, sensory target, and validation need—not unconfirmed final percentages or process.
- Separate desired claim, current evidence, required evidence, and regulatory owner.
- Expose conflicts among product, packaging, cost, channel, and timing rather than hiding them in vague language.

## Change and version control

Capacity, format, viscosity, first-order quantity, target cost, channel, launch market, core claim, and packaging form are high-impact fields. Mark conflicting values as `conflict`; do not choose one silently. Record version, date, and current owner at the top.

## Stop and boundary

- When asked for a final formula, manufacturing process, efficacy proof, regulatory approval, or filing conclusion, retain it as a specialist task and list the accountable owner and evidence required.
- If target user, product format, or core problem cannot be identified, return a definition draft and priority questions only.
- Never invent cost, timing, MOQ, supplier, or test results to complete the brief.

## Completion check

- Product targets are separate from achieved facts.
- Every must-have has a rationale and validation route.
- Formula, claim, packaging, and commercial constraints align or expose conflicts.
- The brief supports the next cross-functional review without becoming a full launch plan.
